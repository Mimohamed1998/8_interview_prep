## Section 1 — Where the Business Stands Today

### 1.1 The Business Is Growing — But the Headline Hides a Fragile Story

Overall, the business has grown at a healthy **7.6% per year** in the median case — that is real, measurable growth. But zoom in, and the picture is more complex. Month-to-month swings have ranged from **-67% to +50%** in a single year, and when we separate what is growing from what is declining, a very different picture emerges.

The growth story is almost entirely explained by volume — more units moving through the network. But below that surface, 24 of your 26 product lines are *shrinking* every year. The aggregate growth is being masked because newer, smaller product lines are being counted alongside the large declining ones.

**The business is not as healthy as top-line growth suggests.**

---

### 1.2 Your Revenue Has Two Dangerous Concentrations

**By Geography: One Province Holds Everything**

Your revenue is spread across 9 provinces and 257 territories — that sounds diversified. It is not. A single province, Province_3, generates **38.5% of your entire $14.2B business** ($5.47B). The top 3 provinces together account for **63%** of revenue. The bottom 4 provinces together share only 18.5%.

> **What this means:** A logistics disruption, competitive entry, or regulatory change in Province_3 alone could wipe out more than a third of annual revenue. This is a board-level risk that deserves explicit contingency planning.

**By Customer: 4,534 Accounts Are Your Business**

Of your 66,736 active outlets (stores, distributors, points of sale), just **4,534 accounts — 6.8% of your customer base — generate 72% of all revenue**. The remaining 62,202 active outlets share the remaining 28%.

On average, each of those 4,534 power accounts is worth approximately **27 times** the revenue of a typical mainstream account. Losing even 500 of them — less than 1% of your active base — would have a more severe financial impact than losing 15,000 mainstream accounts.

> **What this means:** You likely do not have dedicated relationship managers for these accounts today. You should.

---

### 1.3 Almost Half Your Outlets Have Gone Quiet

You have **116,014 outlets on record**. Of those, **49,447 have churned** — they were once buying from you and have stopped. That is a 42.6% loss rate across your known commercial universe.

These are not hypothetical new customers. They already knew your brand, had your products, and at some point stopped ordering. The cost of re-engaging a lapsed customer is typically a fraction of winning a new one — yet this pool of 49,000+ outlets is likely receiving no commercial attention at all.

> **What this means:** Your single largest untapped growth opportunity may not be new market entry — it may be systematically re-engaging the nearly 50,000 outlets you have already lost.

---

### 1.4 Returns Are Rising — and Not Randomly

For every 100 units your business ships, **roughly 11 come back**. That is a 10.7% return rate overall, and it has been rising steadily: from 7.6% in 2015 to 11.4% in 2019 — a **50% relative increase** over four years. In the first quarter of 2020, returns spiked to **24.7%** (1 in 4 units returned), though this coincides with the onset of COVID-19 disruptions.

Returns are not uniformly distributed. Category 2 has a 11.8% return rate; Category 5 is only 6.6%. The data statistically confirms that **which category a customer buys from directly predicts whether they will return it** — this is not random.

| Year | Return Rate |
|------|-------------|
| 2015 | 7.6% |
| 2016 | 9.3% |
| 2017 | 9.8% |
| 2018 | 12.3% |
| 2019 | 11.4% |
| Jan–Mar 2020 | **24.7%** ⚠ |

> **What this means:** A rising return rate is a margin drain that compounds every year. It points to a structural issue — whether in product quality, order accuracy, customer expectations, or category mix. Understanding the root cause in Category 2 is the fastest available margin recovery that does not require a single new sale.

---

### 1.5 Your Business Has a Predictable Annual Rhythm — But Are You Using It?

Demand follows a strong, consistent seasonal pattern every year. **October is your peak month; April is your trough.** This pattern scores 0.936 out of 1.0 on a statistical seasonality scale — "near-maximum certainty." It has been repeating for 5 straight years.

This means demand swings predictably every year, on a fixed cycle. Businesses that plan around this pattern can optimise stock levels, reduce waste in slow months, and never run out during peak. Businesses that ignore it will systematically over-stock in spring and under-stock heading into October — every single year.

> **What this means:** If procurement, production scheduling, and promotional calendars are not currently aligned to the October peak / April trough cycle, correcting this one thing could reduce working capital waste and stock-outs simultaneously — with no new products, no new customers, and no new investment.

## Section 2 — Your Product Portfolio: What's Working and What Is Not

### 2.1 The Portfolio Is Contracting at Its Core

Across your 26 product lines, only 1 is genuinely growing. The **median product line shrank by 27.4% per year** over the past 5 years. This is not normal market fluctuation — it is structural portfolio contraction.

The strategic breakdown:

| Quadrant | What It Means | Key Lines |
|---|---|---|
| **Protect & Invest** | Large revenue, relatively better performance | prod_line11, 12, 14, 17, 18, 22 |
| **Defend** | Your biggest earners — but all shrinking | prod_line3, 5, 10, 7, 6, 19, 4 |
| **Nurture** | Small today, mixed trajectory | prod_line21, 13, 2, 24, others |
| **Review / Exit** | Small and declining fast | prod_line20 (-47%/yr), 23, 25, 16 |

---

### 2.2 Your Biggest Product Line Is Your Biggest Risk

**prod_line3 generates 31.2% of your total revenue** — $4.4B of the $14.2B total. It is your single most important product line by a wide margin.

It is also contracting at **-29.7% per year**.

At this rate of decline:
- **Year 1:** Revenue from this line falls from ~$4.4B to ~$3.1B
- **Year 2:** Falls to ~$2.2B
- **Year 3:** Falls to ~$1.5B

This is not a small adjustment. At current trajectory, prod_line3 alone will lose approximately **$3B in revenue over the next 3 years** — more than the total annual revenue of your second-largest province.

> **What this means:** This is an existential commercial question, not a product team problem. What is causing prod_line3 to decline? Is it a price problem? A distribution problem? A product quality problem? A market structural shift? The answer determines whether you fight to defend it or start managing a controlled transition to replacement products.

---

### 2.3 Your Only Growth Story Is Still Small — But Real

**prod_line17** is growing at **+163.6% per year** from a near-zero starting point. It now represents 2.1% of revenue — small in absolute terms, but the trajectory is exceptional.

This line is behaving like a product in early adoption — the growth rate is too fast to be a mature business, and the revenue base is too small to have plateaued. If it continues, it could meaningfully offset losses elsewhere within 2–3 years.

> **What this means:** prod_line17 deserves dedicated investment and focus — not the same treatment as a declining mid-range line. The growth window is narrow; this kind of trajectory rarely lasts without deliberate commercial fuel behind it.

---

### 2.4 Your Business Is Almost Entirely One Brand

Across 6 brands, one dominant brand accounts for the overwhelming majority of revenue. The Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (a standard measure of concentration — anything above 0.25 is "high dependency") comes in at **0.85 for brands**. This is near-monopoly concentration within your own portfolio.

> **What this means:** Any disruption to your dominant brand — supply chain, quality incident, regulatory action, reputational event — affects virtually all of your $14.2B revenue base simultaneously. Building a genuine second brand is a multi-year programme, but delaying it makes the risk larger every year.

## Section 3 — What Forecasting Can Do for This Business

One of the core findings of this analysis is that your demand patterns — while complex — are statistically forecastable. Here is what that means in practice.

### 3.1 Demand Is Not Smooth — and That Is Exactly Why You Need Better Forecasting

Looking at your 50 products through a statistical lens:

| Demand Type | Products | Volume Share | What It Means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Smooth** (easy to forecast) | 7 products (14%) | 16.5% of volume | Orders come in predictably every month |
| **Irregular** (moderate difficulty) | 29 products (58%) | **69.9% of volume** | Orders mostly regular but with unpredictable spikes |
| **Lumpy** (hardest to forecast) | 14 products (28%) | 13.5% of volume | Orders are patchy — large bursts, then silence |

**70% of your sales volume flows through products that have irregular, spiky demand.** A simple "same as last month" stocking model handles these very poorly — it will systematically run out during spikes and over-stock during quiet periods.

### 3.2 At Individual Store Level, the Problem Is Even More Acute

The analysis above is at a national level. When we look at individual store-product combinations — the level where replenishment decisions are actually made — the picture is dramatically more challenging.

Across the **1.95 million store-product pairs** in your network:

- **97.4% have lumpy, unpredictable demand patterns** at store level
- Only 2.6% have smooth enough demand to be reliably forecast with basic methods

This gap between the national average and operational reality is important: a national-level forecast will look reasonable on paper and generate completely wrong replenishment signals for nearly every store.

### 3.3 What Good Forecasting Would Deliver

With the right forecasting architecture in place — one that uses different methods for different demand types, treats your power accounts separately, and explicitly models the seasonal October/April cycle — the business could expect:

- **Reduced overstock in slow months** by eliminating the "average demand" blind spot for irregular SKUs
- **Fewer stock-outs at peak** by correctly anticipating the October demand surge, not reacting to it
- **Lower safety stock requirements** for your 8% of products with smooth, predictable demand — freeing working capital
- **Higher service levels at power accounts** — the 4,534 outlets that drive 72% of revenue deserve forecast accuracy as a commercial priority, not an average

### 3.4 The Forecasting Architecture the Data Supports

The data is ready for a proper forecasting investment. The analysis recommends:

| Model Type | For | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| SARIMA time series | 7 smooth products | Statistically confirmed: demand is predictable, seasonal, and stationary after one difference |
| Croston / SBA methods | 29 irregular products | Specialist algorithms designed specifically for sporadic demand — standard forecasting will systematically fail these |
| ADIDA + manual review | 14 lumpy products | Aggregation-based approach handles the most unpredictable patterns |

For the 4,534 power outlets: **account-level models**, not cluster averages. Their revenue weight justifies individual treatment.

## Section 4 — Surprising Findings Worth Acting On

### Finding 1: Region_6 Is in Structural Decline

Three of the five worst-performing territories in the entire network are in Region_6 (-34.1%, -29.2%, -24.6% year-on-year). This is not random variation — a formal statistical test (ANOVA, p < 0.0001) confirms that regional performance differences are real and structural.

Something specific has happened in Region_6. This warrants an immediate diagnostic: competitive entry? Distribution failure? Key account losses? Route coverage gaps? The data flags the problem clearly; the field team needs to identify the cause.

### Finding 2: Safety Stock Calculations Are Almost Certainly Wrong

Demand volatility varies enormously across your products. The most volatile products experience monthly demand swings **larger than their own average volume** (a coefficient of variation above 1.0). The most stable products have swings of roughly a third of their average.

If your safety stock (buffer inventory) is calculated using an average figure across all products, it is simultaneously:
- **Over-stocking** your 8% of stable products (tying up working capital unnecessarily)
- **Under-stocking** your 72% of volatile products (chronically exposed to stock-outs)

A high-volatility product needs **3× more safety stock** than a low-volatility product at the same service level. Using one number for both produces the wrong answer for all of them.

### Finding 3: Your Returns System Has Four Anomaly Months That Need Investigating

Beyond the steady-state 10.7% return rate, the data identifies 4 specific months where returns exceeded 22.2% — more than double the normal rate. These are not gradual drift; they are discrete events. Each one likely has a specific, findable cause: a batch quality issue, a pricing correction rollout, a distribution failure, a policy change.

Identifying and resolving the root cause of even one recurrence of these events would have a larger margin impact than reducing the steady-state rate by a full percentage point.

### Finding 4: The Data Foundation Is Clean and Ready

Before any analysis is trustworthy, the underlying data must be complete and correctly structured. Every one of the 6.8 million transaction records was successfully linked to the correct product and territory (100% join rate — an unusually clean result). There are no gaps in the 63-month history. All financial sign conventions are correct. The data passed every quality check.

**This matters because it means the findings above are not data artefacts — they reflect genuine business reality.**
