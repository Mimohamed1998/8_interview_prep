# **1\. Introduction**

Artificial Intelligence (AI) has become a new revolutionary power that is affecting the economic, social, and environmental systems of the world. On the way to the achievement of Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), AI is perceived as a way to improve efficiency, optimal resource use, and innovation. Nevertheless, AI is not sustainable per se, as there are both negative and positive effects. Though AI has the capability to promote clean energy management, minimize garbage, and enhance climate forecasting, it may also enhance energy usage, expand inequality, enhance observation, and create ethical issues.

Such contrasting results give rise to a specific question: why are AI gains in sustainability more in some countries, but not in others? The function of governance is one of the explanations that have been proposed widely. Good governance would provide ethical deployment, regulatory controls, transparency, and responsible innovation, and less governance would enhance the possibility of abuse, inequality, and environmental damage.

This paper examines the possibility that the quality of governance is a moderator between the effect of AI readiness in a country and the consequences of sustainability, using cross-country panel data on 2020-2023.

# **2\. Background and Literature Review.**

# **2.1 AI Readiness and Sustainability results.**

The current literature indicates that sustainability and competitiveness have a strong connection with digital and technological preparedness. According to Dabbous et al. (2023), digital integration, connectivity, and entrepreneurship improve resource efficiency and sustainable development. They highlight that technological readiness will not be effective without a facilitating institutional environment.

On the same note, the digital public services and connectivity enhance energy efficiency in the EU countries according to the findings of Benedetti and Guarini (2023). The authors also point out that digital transformation needs to work together with the green transition by coherent policy frameworks and institutional coordination.

Additional evidence is given by Vasilescu et al. (2025), as they indicate that investments in AI and readiness levels facilitate the effect of renewable-based energy generation and consumption efficiency. The quality of governance, administrative stability and the capacity of the institutions however are of critical importance to the effectiveness of these outcomes.

Collectively, these studies highlight the fact that AI readiness can be used to transform sustainability, although the degree and direction of this influence is contingent on country-specific governance settings.

## **2.2 Governance as a Determinant and a Moderator.**

The quality of governance has been reported to determine the sustainability outcomes as well as digital preparedness. According to Iuga and Socol (2024), the three phenomena institutional effectiveness, regulatory quality and policy stability play a vital role in determining AI readiness in EU member states. Their spatial models show also cross-border spillovers of governance practices, which further supports the systemic governance in the digital transformation.

Governance is even more important in a weaker institutional setting. In a study of AI applied in Africa, Gwagwa et al. (2020) indicate that the benefits of AI to sustainable development are constrained by low accountability and weak institutions. Their results indicate that AI will only enhance inequality, inefficiency, and unethical practices when the governance fails.

These observations correspond to the conceptual argument of this paper: governance can enhance or undermine the AI-sustainability nexus.

## **2.3 Digital-Green Twin Transition.**

Governance is also highlighted as an enabler of digital-green transformation in the literature. According to Diodato et al. (2023), institutional quality, regulatory control, and coherent public investment is what renders AI-driven innovation to be either facilitative or detrimental to sustainability.

This tendency is supported by sectoral evidence. When studying AI in sustainable agriculture, Bhagat et al. (2022) discover that the independent variables associated with strong institutional support, policy stability, and innovation ecosystems in countries that adopted AI in sustainability leadership included the US, China, and developed economies of the EU.

## **2.4 Summary**

Throughout the literature, AI preparedness is always associated with sustainability outcomes, although the correlation is subject to governance quality. Although there is increasing interest, only a limited amount of empirical research aimed, in particular, to test the moderating effect of governance, particularly in a cross-country setting and based on more recent post-2020 data. This paper will address this gap.

# **3\. Research Problem**

Whereas AI readiness is becoming a highly recognized notion of sustainable development, similar countries in terms of AI preparedness level usually demonstrate various sustainability results. Such a discrepancy implies the existence of intervening variables, which are the quality of governance. Although governance has been extensively talked about as an enabler of responsible AI use, the 6 moderating role has not been empirically investigated in a formal cross-country model.

Problem Statement:

Does the quality of governance moderate the connection between AI readiness and sustainability outcomes across nations?

# **4\. Research Objectives**

## **4.1 General Objective**

To test the hypothesis about the moderating role of the quality of governance and the ability of AI preparedness to affect the sustainability outcomes.

# **4.2 Specific Objectives**

* Evaluate the correlation between AI preparedness and sustainability.  
* Assess direct impact of quality of governance and how the same affects sustainability performance.  
* Determine whether the governance quality reinforces the AI-sustainability relationship or not.  
* Compare the trends in the developed and developing nations.

# **5\. Research Question and Hypotheses.**

* RQ1: Does AI preparedness have any influence on sustainability outcomes?  
* RQ2: How does the quality of governance affect the sustainability outcomes?  
* RQ3: Does the quality of governance moderate the AI readiness and sustainability outcomes?

Hypotheses:

* H1: AI preparedness has a positive impact on sustainability performance.  
* H2: Quality of governance has a positive relationship with sustainability.  
* H3: The quality of governance is a moderately positive variable between AI preparedness and sustainability results.

# **6\. Significance of the Study**

This paper can add to the global discussion of digital sustainability because it gives empirical data on the moderating effect of governance. The findings will inform policymakers about whether it is possible to increase the payoffs of AI investment by improving governance systems. The results will also be used by the international development agencies and digital transformation planners as well as sustainability strategists. In the case of developing nations, the research emphasizes the need to institutionalize to take full advantage of AI implementation.

# **7\. Scope and Limitations**

The research relies on secondary data on global datasets of 2020-2023. It is only constrained to countries where full data is available. Since observational data were utilized in the research, the analysis only shows associations and not causation. Lack or incomplete country-level data can be a limitation to the final sample.

# **8\. Methodology**

## **8.1 Study Design**

A cross-country cross-sectional panel quantitative study (2020-2023).

## **8.2 Population**

All countries covered by:

* AI Readiness Index, Oxford Insights.  
* The World Governance Indicators.  
* Sustainable Development Report.

## **8.3 Sample**

* A convenience sample of 15 countries with full data, reflecting a variation of levels of governance and AI readiness:  
* Improved: Finland, Denmark, Singapore, Germany, Japan.  
* Developing: India, Sri Lanka, Brazil, South Africa, Malaysia.  
* Lower governance: Nigeria, Bangladesh, Kenya, Pakistan, Ghana.

## **8.4 Data Collection**

Publicly available datasets of secondary data.

## **8.5 Variables**

*  Independent Variable: AI Readiness Index   
* Moderator: Quality of good governance (WGI average score)  
* Dependent Variable: Sustainability Results (SDG Index score)

## **8.6 Statistical Tools**

* Descriptive statistics  
* Correlation analysis  
* Fixed- and random-effects panel regression.  
* Moderation analysis with interaction term

## **8.7 Ethical Considerations**

All the data are secondary, aggregated, and publicly available. None of the personal or sensitive information is used.

# **9\. Expected Outcomes**

The research will strongly anticipate that the quality of governance would substantially augment the association of AI preparedness and sustainability effects. Strongly governed nations are likely to receive more benefits out of AI investments as compared to weak governance regions that are likely to exhibit limited or none effects. The findings will bring governance as an important lever to a responsible and equitable digital transformation.
